Architecture Demand & Engagement Process

How Architecture manages demand from triage through prioritisation, mobilisation and engagement, with proportionate governance and explicit synchronisation with adjacent organisational processes.

PurposeLifecycleTriagePrioritisationMobilisationServicesDeliverablesSignificanceDependenciesManagementToolchain

1. Purpose

This process defines how Architecture manages demand from initial triage through prioritisation, mobilisation and engagement. Its primary concern is deciding what Architecture should engage with, how important that work is, when it can be mobilised, what service is required and which adjacent organisational processes must be synchronised.

Meetings are for decisions, trade-offs, exceptions and accountability. Normal Architecture work should remain in the Work Package, Azure DevOps, Architecture artefacts and established delivery practices.

Formal governance remains important where required, but the management process is not designed around governance forums. Architectural significance determines the governance route; it does not determine whether useful Architecture work is required.

Sequence matters: Triage determines whether and how Architecture engages. Priority is assigned after triage. Mobilisation then creates or activates the Work Package and establishes a schedulable, resourced engagement.

3. Triage

Triage asks: What Architecture impact does this demand have, and what should happen next? Demand may arrive through the shared organisational Front Door or directly where the request is explicitly for Architecture.

Assess

  • Business objective and proposed scope
  • Affected capabilities, services, applications, platforms, data and integrations
  • Complexity, urgency and material constraints
  • Likely Architecture service and owning team
  • Dependencies on other functions or processes
  • Initial architectural-significance and governance view

Conclude

  • Accept into the Architecture portfolio
  • Refer or hand off to another function
  • Reject / no Architecture engagement
  • Request further assessment where the route cannot yet be determined

The triage record retains the link to the originating SharePoint demand. Accepted Architecture demand becomes an AMO Demand item and moves to portfolio prioritisation.

Gate 1 — Triage Decision: Is this valid Architecture demand and, if so, which service should own the response?

4. Prioritisation

Prioritisation is deliberately separate from triage. Once Architecture accepts demand, the management question becomes how important is this relative to other accepted work?

Value / urgency

Business importance, deadlines, regulatory or operational drivers and consequences of delay.

Dependencies

Programme sequencing, procurement events, business readiness, technology dependencies and specialist contributions.

Capacity

Whether the owning team and required specialists can realistically mobilise the work alongside existing commitments.

Priority should be a portfolio decision rather than an architect silently assigning priority to their own work. The highest priority categories should have an explicit rationale.

5. Mobilisation

Mobilisation turns prioritised demand into executable Architecture work. Forecast dates may exist earlier, but a meaningful schedule requires enough definition to understand the work and its dependencies.

Gate 2 — Mobilised: There is sufficient clarity on scope, inputs, dependencies, resources, deliverables and governance to schedule and execute the engagement. The Work Package exists and Architecture capacity is committed or explicitly forecast.

8. Architectural significance & governance

Not every Architecture engagement requires formal approval. During Triage, and again as the engagement becomes clearer, the architect assesses architectural significance: whether the proposed change, decision or design is sufficiently material to require formal Architecture governance.

Governed Architecture

Where work is architecturally significant, appropriate deliverables and decisions are prepared for formal governance. Architecture Approval Board (AAB) is the approval forum for governed Architecture.

Without formal approval

Not requiring formal governance does not mean Architecture work or documentation is unnecessary. Discovery, Solution Intent, HLD, KDD or other outputs may still be required. AAB may be informed for noting where awareness is useful without seeking approval.

Architectural significance determines the governance route, not whether Architecture engages. Service and deliverables are determined by the needs of the engagement.

9. Synchronisation with adjacent processes

Architecture should not absorb Procurement, Business Architecture or Business Analysis into its own workflow. Triage identifies likely dependencies; Mobilisation makes collaboration explicit in the Work Package, including timing, expected input/output and accountable counterpart where known.

Process / roleWhen relevantSynchronisation points
ProcurementSelection, acquisition, renewal or material change of a supplier, product, platform or commercial technology service.Before procurement requirements or market engagement are fixed; during option assessment; before a preferred commercial option becomes an architectural commitment; and before contract commitments where Architecture conditions must be reflected.
Business ArchitectureDemand affecting strategy, capabilities, value streams, target operating model or organisational responsibilities.Triage/Discovery for strategic context; option development for business alignment; before solution direction is settled where business-design decisions remain unresolved.
Business AnalysisRequired input where a solution has significant business-process impact, including customer journeys, business-team processes or material processes within technology teams.Requirements and process understanding should be sufficiently mature before detailed solution design. Architecture and BA should iterate where requirements, process design and technical options influence one another.

Procurement collaboration

Procurement and Architecture operate as collaborating processes rather than sequential gates. Architecture may shape requirements before market engagement; procurement may expose products, suppliers, constraints and commercial options requiring further Architecture assessment. A commercially preferred option does not become architecturally committed merely because it is commercially preferred.

Business Architecture contribution

Business Architecture contributes where demand depends on, or materially changes, strategic intent, capabilities, value streams or the operating model. This may precede solution Architecture or run alongside Discovery.

Business Analysis contribution

For solutions with significant process impact, Architecture requires sufficiently clear requirements and process design to make sound decisions. Early Architecture may proceed with hypotheses and open questions, but detailed design should not silently substitute architectural assumptions for unresolved business requirements.

Management rule: material dependencies should be identified during Triage where possible and made explicit during Mobilisation. Demand should not enter detailed Architecture work with a significant unresolved dependency hidden outside the Work Package.

10. Internal management rhythm

The process should remain deliberately lean. Architecture practices perform the work; management forums deal with demand decisions, portfolio trade-offs and exceptions.

ActivityCadencePurposeMain output
Architecture Demand TriageWeekly, 30–45 minQualify new demand and determine the Architecture route.AMO Demand updated; service/team/owner and accept/refer/reject outcome.
Portfolio Prioritisation & MobilisationWeekly or fortnightly, 45–60 minPrioritise accepted work, agree sequencing, resolve capacity trade-offs and decide what can mobilise.Priority, Work Package/ADO actions, dates and resource intent.
Delivery / Operations ReviewWeekly, 20–30 minReview exceptions rather than every Demand item.Interventions, actions and escalations.
Portfolio / Status ReviewMonthlyReview demand, capacity, Health and trends.Published AMO Status Report and management actions.

Exception controls

Triaged without priority

Accepted work has not entered portfolio prioritisation.

Prioritised too long

Demand is ageing without mobilisation.

Mobilising without Work Item

No ADO Epic/Feature exists for executable work.

Mobilising without owner/allocation

The engagement is not genuinely schedulable.

Significance not assessed

No clear view exists on whether formal governance is required.

Adjacent dependency unresolved

Required Procurement, Business Architecture or Business Analysis contribution has no clear synchronisation point or owner.

11. Toolchain ownership

SharePoint

External Demand / Front Door

Owns the originating request and organisational demand intake.

AMO

Architecture portfolio

Owns service, phase/status, priority, team, owner, dates, Health, resource allocation, roadmap and management reporting.

Azure DevOps

Execution

Owns the Architecture Epic/Feature, deliverable work items and detailed work tracking.

Architecture repositories

Architecture knowledge

Own formal Discovery, Solution Intent, HLD, KDD and approved Architecture artefacts.

Ownership rule: “Should / when / who is Architecture doing this?” belongs in AMO. “What work are we doing to deliver it?” belongs in Azure DevOps. “What is the Architecture?” belongs in the Architecture repository. “Who asked for it?” belongs at the Front Door.
