Architecture Services & Engagement Process

A clear, proportionate operating model for routing demand, prioritising Architecture work, mobilising engagements, producing design artefacts, governing decisions and closing work.

1. Purpose

Architecture operates within a wider organisational demand process. Some demand is cross-functional and arrives via a shared Front Door; some demand is explicitly for Architecture and can be owned end-to-end by the Architecture team.

Demand → Front Door → Architecture Triage → Prioritise → Mobilise & Define Work → Execute → Review → Approve / Govern → Close

The process must also allow Architecture to refer demand elsewhere or continue while recording material external dependencies.

4. End-to-End Architecture Process

The process is intentionally stable even though the size of the engagement and artefacts produced may vary significantly.

1. DemandShared Front Door or direct Architecture request.
2. Triage / AIAImpact, complexity, ownership and route.
3. PrioritiseSet relative priority after triage and decide when mobilisation should begin.
4. Mobilise & Define EngagementCreate the Architecture Work Package, agree scope and establish executable schedule and resource commitment.
5. ExecuteAnalyse, decide, design, advise or assure.
6. Review & SocialiseSMEs, BAs, Engineering, Data, Security and stakeholders.
7. Approve / GovernFormal decision and conditions.
8. CloseStore artefacts, assign actions and end delivery ownership.

5. Process 1 — Architecture Triage & AIA

Triage asks: What Architecture impact does this demand have, and what should happen next?

Inputs

  • Demand / business objective and proposed scope
  • Affected capabilities, services, applications, platforms, data and integrations
  • Delivery timescales
  • Known security, regulatory and risk considerations
  • Relevant existing designs or decisions

Process

  1. Understand the demand.
  2. Identify Architecture impact and affected domains.
  3. Assess complexity — Small / Medium / Large.
  4. Identify risks and dependencies.
  5. Determine whether Architecture is the correct owner.
  6. Identify other teams/functions required.
  7. Select the required Architecture service.
  8. Estimate ROM effort and likely deliverables.
  9. Identify the likely governance route.
  10. Conclude the triage decision.

Deliverable — Architecture Impact Assessment (AIA)

The AIA records the demand summary, Architecture impact, complexity, affected domains, indicative effort, recommended service, likely deliverables, dependencies, referrals, required SMEs and likely governance route.

Gate 1 — Triage Decision: Is this valid Architecture demand that should enter the AMO portfolio? If yes, the item leaves Triage and receives a portfolio priority. If no, it is referred, rejected or closed.

6. Process 2 — Architecture Consultancy

Architecture Consultancy is a time-and-materials / advisory service. Architecture provides expertise but does not assume design or delivery ownership.

Typical activities

Workshops, solution advice, options analysis, standards interpretation, informal review and delivery support.

Typical outputs

Advice, recommendations, workshop outputs, options and KDDs where a material decision is required.

Control

If consultancy becomes substantive Architecture analysis or design ownership, it should be converted into a formal Architecture Work Package and mobilised accordingly.

7. Process 3 — Prioritisation & Mobilisation

Once triage confirms valid Architecture demand, AMO assigns relative portfolio priority. Priority answers how important is this relative to other accepted demand? It is deliberately separate from the triage assessment itself.

AIA:
What help do we think is required?
Architecture Work Package:
What exactly have we agreed to do?

9. Process 5 — Review & Socialisation

Architecture outcomes should be developed and challenged with the appropriate community before formal governance. The goal is not simply to circulate a finished document for comments, but to show that it has been shaped with the right expertise.

Business & Delivery

Business Analysts, Product, business stakeholders and Engineering.

Architecture

Solution Architecture, Enterprise Architecture, Business Architecture, Data and Integration Architecture.

Specialist Functions

Security, Cloud/Infrastructure, Platforms, Operations, Data Office and relevant SMEs.

Gate 3 — Ready for Governance

  • Required SMEs have been engaged.
  • Material concerns have been addressed or explicitly recorded.
  • Dependencies are resolved sufficiently to proceed.
  • Significant decisions are documented.
  • Requirements are adequately addressed.
  • Exceptions are identified.
  • Architecture artefacts are sufficiently complete.

11. Process 7 — Closure & Handover

An Architecture engagement should have a defined end. Closure occurs when the agreed Architecture obligation has been met and any residual ownership is explicit.

  • Agreed deliverables are complete.
  • Decisions are recorded.
  • Required governance is complete.
  • Residual actions have owners.
  • Dependencies are appropriately handed over.
  • Architecture artefacts are stored or published.
  • Delivery teams understand relevant Architecture constraints.
  • Architecture delivery ownership has ended.

13. Operating Principles

One Architecture Front Door

Architecture demand is assessed consistently regardless of where it originates.

Integrate with Wider Demand Management

Architecture can own direct demand or contribute to a wider enterprise Front Door process without duplicating it.

Route Rather Than Absorb

Demand that belongs elsewhere should be referred rather than becoming Architecture work by default.

Make Dependencies Explicit

Architecture ownership does not imply ownership of Data, Security, Platforms, Engineering or other specialist responsibilities.

Prioritise Before Mobilising

Triage decides whether and how Architecture should engage. Portfolio priority is assigned after that decision and determines when mobilisation should begin.

Define Before Committing

The Architecture Work Package establishes what Architecture is actually agreeing to deliver and enables a credible executable schedule.

Proportionate Architecture

The appropriate outcome may be advice, strategy, a KDD, HLD Lite, full HLD or assurance only.

Govern Decisions, Not Paperwork

Governance provides confidence in material Architecture decisions and risk rather than merely approving documents.

Explicit Ownership & Closure

Every engagement should have a clear owner, defined outputs and an identifiable end point.

14. Implementation — Tools, Data Ownership & Operational Flow

The logical process is implemented through a small number of tools, each with a deliberately narrow purpose. The intent is to minimise duplicate entry, avoid parallel status tracking and make ownership of information clear.

SharePoint qualifies the demand → AMO prioritises, mobilises and plans Architecture demand → Azure DevOps manages the committed Work Package and execution → Architecture Libraries hold the durable Architecture outputs.

  • Execution estimate

14.2 Gate 1 — Triage to Architecture Demand

Decision: Is this valid Architecture demand that should enter the AMO planning pipeline?

Architecture triage remains in SharePoint because ROM, Architecture impact and initial scope assessment are part of Front Door qualification. Demand that is rejected, closed or referred elsewhere does not need to enter AMO or ADO.

SharePoint owns

Original demand, triage outcome, Architecture impact, complexity, ROM, likely service, likely deliverables and referral/dependency information.

→
AMO receives accepted demand

Demand ID/link, title, Business Area, Initiative where relevant, service, priority, planning dates, Architecture owner/team and enough information to plan capacity and mobilisation.

14.3 Demand, priority & resource planning

AMO is the control point for the Architecture portfolio. It answers:

What should we do?

Accepted demand, relative priority and target timing.

Can we do it?

Available capacity versus forecast Architecture demand.

Who will do it?

Proposed and confirmed Architecture allocation.

AMO should not become a duplicate delivery tracker. Once an engagement is mobilised, detailed execution belongs in ADO; AMO retains the management-level phase, status, Health, dates and portfolio information required for departmental reporting.

14.4 Gate 2 — Prioritise and Mobilise Architecture Demand

Decision: Triage is complete, relative priority is agreed, the Architecture Work Package is being defined, and the engagement can now be scheduled and resourced.

Mobilisation creates or activates the ADO Architecture Work Package. That Work Package turns the triage outcome into a concrete engagement definition. Once it exists, AMO can establish and maintain the executable schedule and named resource allocation against that defined work. Priority comes first; Work Package definition and scheduling are completed together during mobilisation.

Customer / engagement control

Architecture Work Package — ADO

Answers: What exactly have we agreed to do?

  • Objective and refined scope
  • Required inputs and dependencies
  • Architecture owner
  • Expected deliverables
  • Execution estimate
  • Target dates and governance route
Portfolio / management control

AMO

Answers: When can we do it, and with whom?

  • Priority and portfolio sequence
  • Forecast demand / ROM
  • Named and proposed resource
  • Allocation by planning month
  • Available capacity
  • Planned dates, resource gaps and conflicts
The Work Package controls the individual engagement. AMO controls the Architecture portfolio.

14.9 Information hand-off rules

Reference upstream data — do not copy it

Carry the Front Door / triage reference and only the stable conclusions needed downstream.

Move ownership only at a process gate

SharePoint owns qualification; AMO owns accepted demand, prioritisation, mobilisation and portfolio planning; ADO owns the committed Work Package and detailed execution; Architecture Libraries own durable outputs.

Allow ROM and execution estimate to differ

The SharePoint ROM supports triage/planning. The ADO estimate is refined once scope is better understood.

Keep identifiers linked end-to-end

Front Door ID → Architecture triage record → AMO Demand → ADO Work Package → ADO deliverable → Architecture Library artefact.

14.11 Implementation principles

Enter Once

Do not maintain the same narrative scope, status or deliverable detail in multiple tools.

Reference, Don't Replicate

Carry IDs and links downstream so users can navigate to source information.

Separate Planning from Execution

AMO manages portfolio, mobilisation, schedule and resource forecast. ADO manages the committed Work Package and detailed execution.

Separate Work from Architecture

ADO tracks production of the artefact. The Architecture Library owns the artefact itself.

Use Existing Organisational Governance

Architecture deliverables are governed through established organisational Architecture processes rather than duplicated here.

Close the Work, Retain the Architecture

ADO work items can close once obligations are complete; governed Architecture remains discoverable in the libraries.
